# 0027: Acting on who is present

Date: 2026-11-24
Status: Accepted (run A built: every turn is decided, recorded and shown; run B built 2026-11-24: a guest's turn is restricted in code; `[people] enabled = false` by default)

## Context

Lucy runs in a shared home. Since ADR 0021 every voice utterance carries a speaker tag, and since ADR 0026 (stage 3) a glance names the enrolled faces in view. Neither changes anything yet: both are labels, "never an authorisation". But Lucy holds one person's calendar, reminders, email (MCP, ADR 0022), memory and profile, and anyone in the room can ask for them; anything a visitor says is learned as if the owner had said it. The user wants a privacy default: the owner gets everything, as today; anyone else is a guest who gets lookups and small conveniences, and leaves nothing behind.

The orchestrator is stateless (ADR 0004): everything a turn does is derived from the event log, so that a turn and its pre-fill (`next_turn_prefix`, ADR 0017) agree and a restart changes nothing. Who a turn is with must be derived the same way.

## Decision

### Who a turn is with (run A, built)

- **One owner.** `[people] owner` names the one person who gets everything: the name their voice and face are enrolled under, checked with the same name rules (`lucy.core.names`, moved out of the voice enrolment tool so config can use them). Anyone else is a guest, including an enrolled family member, who is recognised by name but treated as a guest for now. Startup warns when the owner is enrolled neither as a voice (`<workspace>/speakers/NAME`) nor as a face (the face book): then only typed turns, and those carried over from them, are the owner's.
- **The rules** (`lucy.core.presence.PresencePolicy`), from the session's events up to and including the turn's `UserMessage`, first that decides wins:
  1. **Typed** (`source` "text", `lucy chat`) is the owner. A memory correction (`lucy memory correct`, its own session) counts as typed.
  2. **Voice tag**: the owner's name is the owner; any other enrolled name is a guest with that name. Names compare ignoring case.
  3. **Glance**, when there is no voice tag: the owner in view and nobody else (no other recognised person, no unknown face) is the owner; faces in view that do not include the owner are a guest, named when exactly one recognised person and no unknown face is in view; the owner together with others decides nothing (anyone of them may be speaking), nor does an empty glance.
  4. **Carry over**: otherwise the latest earlier message of the same session that rules 1 to 3 decided is kept, if it is at most `carry_minutes` (default 5) older than this message; an older one, or none, makes a guest.
- **Carrying never chains.** Only decisions of rules 1 to 3 are carried, so a run of untagged turns stays the owner's for `carry_minutes` after the last real decision and then becomes a guest's, however close together the turns are. The search stops at the latest real decision: if it is too old, older ones are not looked at.
- **Event time, not wall time.** The age is the difference of the two messages' own timestamps (`Event.ts`), so the decision recomputed from the same log later, by run B, a check or a test, is the same.
- **Proactive turns decide nothing.** A `TimerFired` (a reminder, a timer, an enrolment outcome) has no speaker, gets no decision and no record, and is skipped when carrying: a reminder spoken between two user turns neither breaks nor renews the carry.
- **An answer to a confirmation follows the same rules.** "Yes" to a pending dangerous call (ADR 0006) is an ordinary user message: decided by its own tag or glance, else carried.
- **Voice and chat in one session** (`lucy chat` and `lucy voice` with the same `--session`): a typed message makes the following untagged voice turns the owner's for `carry_minutes`. This is a known consequence of rule 1 plus carrying, accepted because typing at Lucy's terminal is the strongest sign of the owner available; it means someone who speaks right after the owner typed is treated as the owner unless their voice or face says otherwise.
- **The record.** With `[people] enabled`, the orchestrator writes `TurnWith(role, name, basis, message_id)` at the start of every user turn, before the turn's `UserMessage` (after a `ToolsConfigured`, if one is due), with the turn's `turn_id`. `role` is "owner" or "guest"; `name` the recognised person (the owner's configured name for a typed turn; None for an unknown guest); `basis` is "typed", "voice", "face", "carried" or "none". Like `ToolsConfigured` (ADR 0025, amendment 2026-11-17) it is persisted only: never published on the bus, never a message, not content for memory extraction (`CONTENT_EPISODE_TYPES`), ignored by history and clients; forgetting a turn deletes it with the turn. What acts on who is present reads this record (`lucy.core.presence.turn_with(events, turn_id)`) and does not decide again. Old logs load unchanged; a turn without a record (feature off, or before it existed) has no decision.
- **Shown.** The turn trace has `presence`; the voice timings have `turn_with`, `turn_with_name` and `turn_with_basis`; in `lucy voice` and `lucy listen` a guest's turn is marked after the heard words, dim on a terminal: `you> what's the time  (guest)` or `you (Asha)> hello  (guest: Asha)`. Owner turns are not marked. The voice client asks the orchestrator (`Orchestrator.turn_with`, through `LucyApp.turn_with`) before publishing; that query decides from the same log the turn will read and stores nothing.
- **Off switch.** `[people] enabled = false` (the default) decides nothing and records nothing: the event log, the prompts and the screen are exactly as before.
- **Run A changed no prompt and no tool**; run B (below) acts on the record.

### What a guest turn means (run B, built)

Everything below applies only to a turn whose record says "guest"; the owner's turns, and every turn with `[people]` off, are built exactly as before (tested: same log, same prompts).

- **No profile, no standing instructions, no memories.** Retrieval is not called at all (not called and dropped); the stable system message is the persona alone. Standing instructions are left out too: they are the owner's ("call me Yogi").
- **History: only the current guest run.** The prompt's history is the exchanges of the turns in the current run of guest turns (`presence.guest_run`): walking the `TurnWith` records back from the latest, while each is a guest's with the same name (ignoring case; an unnamed guest is its own name). An owner's turn, or a different guest, ends the run; proactive turns have no record and neither end nor join it (and their words, often the owner's reminders, are not shown). Chosen over "every guest exchange in the window" because an unnamed guest cannot be told from another one, so a later guest could otherwise hear what an earlier visitor said, and because a guest coming back after the owner spoke has usually moved on; the cost is that a guest who is interrupted by one owner turn loses their earlier context. Filtering is by turn id, so nothing the owner said, nothing Lucy told the owner and no tool result of an owner's turn (email text) reaches a guest's prompt. Stale exchanges (ADR 0025) are judged on the whole window first, then filtered.
- **The per-turn line** (in the per-turn block with the date and the glance's line, never in the stable prefix; `presence.guest_line`): "You are talking with a guest, Asha, not Yogesh. You can look things up, tell the time, tell the weather, set a timer and say what the camera shows. Anything about Yogesh (Yogesh's calendar, reminders, email, or what you know about Yogesh) is only for Yogesh: if asked, say so briefly and kindly, and do not guess." Only capabilities whose tools are both in `guest_tools` and configured are listed. The name is used when known; the owner is named, never given a pronoun.
- **The system prompt is the same persona** for both roles, including guidance about tools a guest is not offered. Left as it is: the tools are not offered and are refused in code, the per-turn line says what a guest can do, and a second persona would cost a second cached prefix. Not measured whether the stray guidance makes the model try owner tools more often (the refusal makes it harmless).
- **Tools offered**: `[people] guest_tools` that are configured, in configured order. With dynamic tools (ADR 0025) the guest's selector is the owner's limited to those tools (`ToolSelector.limited_to`): the same groups, core and keywords, so `more_tools` lists and switches on only groups that contain a guest tool (default: web, time, reminders with only `set_timer`, vision) and answers "No such group" for the rest; keywords switch groups on from the guest run's messages only.
- **Tools enforced**: in the step loop and in `_run_tool`, a configured tool outside the guest's set is refused before any confirmation, guard or run, with the result "Not run: that is only available to Yogesh." (`is_error`), which the model relays. No `ToolStarted` is logged for it. This covers MCP tools, the calendar, reminders, `cancel_reminder`, and the voice and face enrolment tools, so **enrolment is owner-only**; `guest_tools` may never list the enrolment tools (config error), since enrolling decides who the owner is.
- **A confirmation pending from the owner's turn** cannot be answered by a guest's turn: it is ended with the new outcome `not_owner` (`ConfirmationResolved`), the owner's call is not put in the guest's prompt or turn (no code-authored tool call is logged, unlike other outcomes), and nothing runs. If the guest's words were a yes or a no, code says "Only Yogesh can answer that, so nothing was changed." and the turn ends without a model call; otherwise the turn goes on as a normal guest turn. A question asked in a guest's turn (only possible for a guest tool, e.g. the private-data guard) may be answered by a guest; the owner can answer any question. The private-data guard and risk tiers are unchanged for the owner.
- **`set_timer` by a guest** runs as usual. Its result names the timer's id ("Timer t7 set for 5 minutes; it ends at 18:05"), which says how many reminders and timers exist, a small leak accepted. Listing (`reminders`) and cancelling (`cancel_reminder`) are owner-only, so a guest cannot stop the timer they set. The timer is delivered like any reminder (proactive, see open points), with any of the owner's reminders due at the same moment.
- **Nothing learned**: memory extraction reads each turn's `TurnWith`; a guest's turn is marked extracted without a model call ("a guest's turn", so it is never retried) and guest turns' episodes are left out of the "earlier conversation" context shown when extracting the owner's turns. The other background jobs (entity summaries, reflection, profile rewrite, decay, merges) read statements, not episodes, so nothing of a guest's turn reaches them. The episodes stay in the log.
- **The pre-fill** (`next_turn_prefix`) cannot know who speaks next; it assumes the next turn is with whoever the latest record says (as an untagged turn within `carry_minutes` would be), and shapes the prefix as that role's turn will: for a guest, the persona alone, the guest run's history and the guest's tools (tested to be the exact start of the guest's next prompt). Costs, from ADR 0017's about 850 tokens/s pre-fill, not measured here: owner to guest, the cached prefix diverges right after the persona, and the guest's prompt (persona, the few guest tool specs, a short guest history) is small, so the miss costs roughly 1 to 2 s; guest to owner, the owner's whole prefix (instructions, profile, the owner's tools, up to `history_tokens` (1,200) of history; about 3,000 to 4,000 tokens) is pre-filled again, about 4 s, once, after which the post-reply pre-fill is the owner's again. A misjudged role in the pre-fill costs the same one miss and changes nothing else.

### Security

This amends ADR 0021's "a voice match is never an authorisation" and ADR 0026's same rule for faces: matches now choose what Lucy offers. They are still **not authentication**: a recording of the owner's voice or a photo of their face passes as the owner, and an unrecognised owner (a cold, a dark room) is a guest. So this is a privacy default for a shared home (a visitor does not hear the owner's calendar or teach Lucy things in the owner's name), not a security boundary against someone who wants in. Nothing that matters is gated on it alone: destructive tools still ask, by voice, before they run; email still goes through the private-data guard (ADR 0022). Typing at Lucy's terminal counts as the owner because the terminal is the owner's machine.

## Consequences

- With `[people]` on, each user turn adds one small record to the log (about 200 bytes) and one read of the recent window before a voice turn is shown (the same read the turn makes).
- A misjudged turn is visible as it happens ("(guest)" next to words the owner said) and afterwards in the trace and timings, so the rules and `carry_minutes` can be tuned from real use before run B makes them matter.
- A guest's turn is restricted as above; a misjudged owner (a cold, a dark room) gets a guest's turn and has to type, show their face or say it again.
- First run: with `[people]` on and the owner enrolled neither by voice nor face, every voice turn is a guest's and a guest cannot enrol, so the startup warning says to enrol from `lucy chat` (typed is the owner): "remember my face", after which voice turns with the face in view are the owner's and "remember my voice" works by voice. The rules are not weakened for it.
- In `lucy listen` the guest marker is shown (the listen UI passes it through); the plain speaker tag is still not shown there, as before.

### Open points

- **Reminders spoken aloud while a guest is present** (still open after run B; delivery unchanged). A due reminder is a proactive turn with no speaker; it is spoken to whoever is in the room. Whether to hold it while the last decision was a guest's, or say only "you have a reminder", is open.
- **Family members.** An enrolled family member is a guest today. Once memory is per person, they could get their own memories and reminders; what they should get, and whether they may enrol others, is open.
- **Not measured in run B**: the live behaviour of the chat model on guest turns (declining kindly, not inventing personal details) and the real pre-fill cost of a role switch; the restrictions themselves hold by construction and are tested with fakes. Measured 2026-11-24 (`checks/tool_choice/guest.py`, gemma4:12b-mlx, 105 turns): no planted owner detail reached a guest and no owner tool ran; with the guest line naming voice and face enrolment as the owner's and asking to say "it is only for" the owner, 102/105 passed (3 "I don't know anything about Sam" denials; before the wording change, "I will recognize your voice" 3 of 6 times and declines that claimed Lucy had no calendar). The pre-fill cost is still not measured.
- **The carry-over window is a guess.** 5 minutes was chosen, not measured; the timings and traces of real use should decide it.
- **Unknown voice with the owner's face.** An utterance whose voice matched nobody falls to the glance, so the owner in view alone makes it the owner's; whether an unmatched voice should itself count against the owner is open.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Deciding in the voice client.** The client cannot see typed turns in a shared session, and the decision would not be in the log for run B and memory extraction; the orchestrator decides from the log and the client only asks.
- **Recomputing in every consumer instead of a record.** Every consumer would need the policy and its configuration, and a changed `owner` or `carry_minutes` would re-judge old turns; the record keeps what was decided when it happened. The rules stay pure, so a check can recompute and compare.
- **Carrying the latest decision of any kind.** A carried decision carrying itself would keep an untagged conversation the owner's forever.
- **Several owners or per-person permissions now.** Not needed for the shared-home default, and better designed once memory is per person.
